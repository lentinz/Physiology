    About this model

    This is a simplified, qualitative model of the left ventricle, the arteries and the veins. It is built to show direction and relative size of change, not to predict values for a real person. A resting adult starts at HR 70 bpm, EDV 120 mL, ESV 50 mL, SV 70 mL, EF 58%, CO 4.9 L/min and BP 120/80 mmHg, which matches the representative values in the lecture.

    • Cardiac output equals venous return in the steady state. The model finds the filling pressure where what the veins return equals what the heart pumps.
    • Preload (filling pressure, set by venous return) moves the heart along the end-diastolic pressure-volume curve. Contractility changes the slope of the end-systolic curve. Afterload is the arterial pressure the ventricle must reach to eject. Heart rate sets the time available for filling.
    • BP = cardiac output × resistance. Pulse pressure rises with stroke volume and with faster ejection.
    • The exercise slider moves heart rate, contractility, muscle pump, venoconstriction, breathing and resistance together toward a maximal effort. Maximal heart rate is set to 190 bpm. Stroke volume plateaus in the middle of the range, as in untrained adults (Powers Ch. 9). Trained athletes keep a larger stroke volume.
    • The reflex switch adds a simple baroreflex: a fall in mean pressure raises heart rate, contractility, venous tone and resistance.
    • Right-heart, lung and valve details are not modeled. Heart failure scenarios are shown only as contractility and stiffness changes.
