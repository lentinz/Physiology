About this model

This is a simplified, qualitative model of how the heart generates and conducts its electrical signal, for normal physiology and exercise. It shows direction and relative size of change, not values for a real person. Taylor, the typical person, starts at about 70 bpm with a PR interval of 170 ms, QRS of 90 ms and QT of about 370 ms.

  • Heart rate comes from the sinoatrial node. With no nerve input the intrinsic rate is about 100 bpm. Vagal (parasympathetic) activity slows it and sympathetic activity speeds it. The model sets the slope of phase 4 from the balance of the two.
  • The exercise slider follows the textbook sequence: vagal tone is withdrawn first, then sympathetic activity rises. This is a simplification. White and Raven (2014) argue that the vagus stays active throughout exercise and that the balance shifts gradually from vagal to sympathetic dominance. The sharp hand-off here is for teaching.
  • Drew, Taylor and Elliot appear on the Autonomic control tab. They are three illustrative 30-year-olds who differ in resting vagal tone (modeled to follow the chronic adaptations in Module 3, Lab 5: lower resting heart rate with training, maximal heart rate unchanged). The recovery curves are illustrative and are not tuned to real data; the size of the difference in recovery between people varies in the literature (see Imai 1994 for one study of athletes).
  • Current bars are qualitative sketches of when each current is active. They are not measured amplitudes.
  • The ECG is built from the timing of conduction (PR), ventricular depolarization (QRS) and repolarization (QT), and the QT shortens as heart rate rises. It represents one lead (similar to lead II).
  • Disease states (arrhythmias, electrolyte disturbances) are not part of this version.

What is and is not sourced

Phase names and the ion currents follow the lecture slides. Normal ranges in the Overview (PR, QRS, QT, resting potentials) are typical textbook values and are not taken from one source. Everything for Drew, Elliot and the recovery curves is a modeled illustration, and the model's parameters are tuned by hand.
