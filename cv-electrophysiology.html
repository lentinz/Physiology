    About this model

    This is a simplified, qualitative model of how the heart generates and conducts its electrical signal. It shows direction and relative size of change, not values for a real person. A resting adult starts at about 70 bpm with a PR interval of 170 ms, QRS of 90 ms and QT of about 370 ms, within the normal ranges in the lecture.

    • Heart rate comes from the sinoatrial node. With no nerve input the intrinsic rate is about 100 bpm. Parasympathetic (vagal) activity slows it and sympathetic activity speeds it. The model sets the slope of phase 4 from these two inputs.
    • The exercise slider withdraws vagal tone first, then raises sympathetic activity, as described in Powers Ch. 9. Maximal rate is about 190 bpm.
    • Action potentials are drawn with the phases and the main currents named in the lecture. Current traces are qualitative sketches of when each current is active, not measured amplitudes.
    • The ECG is built from the timing of conduction (PR), ventricular depolarization (QRS) and repolarization (QT). The QT shortens as heart rate rises. It represents one lead (similar to lead II).
    • Raising extracellular K⁺ depolarizes the resting potential of the ventricular myocyte, which is shown only as a brief extension. Arrhythmias are shown as patterns, not as mechanisms in detail.
